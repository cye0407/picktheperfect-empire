Best Purple Potatoes

Purple potatoes aren't novelties. They're heritage varieties with measurable nutritional differences and distinct culinary properties.

Published: 2026-01-23

What Makes Purple Potatoes Different

The purple color comes from anthocyanins — the same antioxidants in blueberries and red cabbage. This affects more than just appearance.

Antioxidant content

Purple potatoes have 2-3 times the antioxidants of white varieties. The anthocyanins that create purple color are the source of this difference.

Texture range

Purple potatoes range from waxy to starchy. Color doesn't determine texture — different purple varieties have different starch contents and cooking properties.

Flavor profile

Many purple potatoes have slightly nutty, earthy flavor distinct from standard varieties. This isn't universal — some taste similar to conventional potatoes.

Color stability

The purple color is water-soluble. Boiling leaches anthocyanins into cooking water. Roasting or steaming preserves color better.

Types of Purple Potatoes

Purple Majesty

Deep purple throughout with high starch content. It mashes well but turns lavender when mixed with dairy. Good for distinctive presentations.

Tradeoff: color can be off-putting in traditional dishes. Works better in applications where purple is expected.

Adirondack Blue

Medium-starch purple with good all-around performance. It holds color reasonably well and adapts to multiple cooking methods.

Tradeoff: less intense purple than specialty varieties. The practical versatility comes with less dramatic color.

Purple fingerlings

Small purple varieties with firm, waxy texture. They roast exceptionally well and hold shape in salads. The small size intensifies flavor.

Tradeoff: lower yield per plant. Heritage fingerling genetics prioritize quality over quantity.

Cooking Considerations for Purple Potatoes

Color preservation

Acidic ingredients (vinegar, lemon) help preserve purple color. Alkaline conditions (baking soda) turn them blue-green. Plan preparations accordingly.

Mixing with other varieties

Purple potatoes bleed color into surrounding ingredients. In mixed dishes, this can muddy appearance. Keep them separate or embrace the color mixing intentionally.

Cooking method impact

Roasting and steaming preserve color and nutrients. Boiling leaches both into water. Microwaving preserves color well but affects texture.

Nutritional Considerations

Antioxidant retention

The anthocyanins are highest in the skin and just below it. Peeling removes most nutritional advantage. Cook with skins on when possible.

Cooking temperature effects

High heat degrades some anthocyanins. Gentler cooking preserves more nutrients but may not achieve desired texture for all applications.

Common Purple Potato Mistakes

Expecting uniform behavior

"Purple potato" describes color, not cooking properties. Some are starchy, some waxy. Some taste distinctive, some taste conventional. Color alone doesn't predict performance.

Boiling and discarding water

Boiling leaches the valuable anthocyanins you chose purple potatoes for. If you must boil, use minimal water or save the cooking liquid for other uses.

Treating them as gimmicks

Purple potatoes are heritage varieties bred for specific properties. They're not dyed or genetically modified. They predate most modern potato varieties.

Growing Purple Potatoes

Disease resistance

Some purple varieties have excellent disease resistance. Others are more susceptible than modern commercial varieties. Check specific variety traits.

Yield expectations

Many purple potatoes are heritage varieties optimized for quality rather than yield. Expect lower pounds per plant than modern commercial types.
