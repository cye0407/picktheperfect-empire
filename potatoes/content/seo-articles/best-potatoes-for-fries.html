Best Potatoes for French Fries

Crispy fries require high-starch, low-moisture potatoes. The variety determines the ceiling — technique can't compensate for wrong potato choice.

Published: 2026-01-23

What Actually Determines Fry Quality

Commercial fry operations use specific varieties for measurable reasons. Home cooks benefit from the same logic.

Starch content

High starch is non-negotiable for crispy fries. Starch granules absorb oil and create the crisp exterior. Low-starch waxy potatoes stay limp regardless of oil temperature or frying time.

Moisture level

Dry potatoes fry crisper. Wet potatoes steam internally before browning, resulting in soggy fries with pale color. The difference is structural, not fixable with longer cooking.

Sugar content

Low sugar is critical. High-sugar potatoes brown too quickly, burning before the interior cooks through. This is why potatoes stored cold (which converts starch to sugar) make poor fries.

Shape and size consistency

Long, cylindrical potatoes cut into uniform fries that cook evenly. Irregular shapes mean inconsistent results — some burnt, some raw.

Potato Types That Excel for Frying

Russet potatoes

Russets are the commercial standard. Very high starch, very low moisture, and low sugar content create the ideal frying profile. They deliver consistent crispiness.

Tradeoff: mild flavor. Russet fries depend heavily on salt and seasoning for taste.

Kennebec potatoes

Kennebecs balance high starch with slightly more flavor than russets. Many restaurants prefer them for this reason. They fry crisp and golden.

Tradeoff: less widely available than russets. Regional supply varies.

Maris Piper (European markets)

Popular in Europe for frying, Maris Pipers have excellent starch content and fry exceptionally crisp. They're the UK chippy standard.

Tradeoff: difficult to find in North American markets.

Common Frying Mistakes

Using waxy or all-purpose potatoes

Red potatoes, Yukon Golds, and other medium-starch varieties make acceptable fries at best. They lack the starch content for maximum crispiness. Commercial operations avoid them for this reason.

Frying potatoes straight from cold storage

Cold storage converts starch to sugar. Even ideal varieties make dark, burnt fries if used directly from refrigeration. Store potatoes at room temperature for frying.

Treating all russets as equivalent

"Russet" describes skin type, not specific variety. Different russet varieties have different starch levels and moisture content. Russet Burbank is the gold standard; generic russets vary.

The Double-Fry Technique

Even perfect potato choice benefits from proper technique. Double-frying (low temp first, high temp second) delivers the best results by cooking the interior before crisping the exterior.

But technique can't override variety limitations. A waxy potato double-fried will still be less crisp than a russet single-fried.
