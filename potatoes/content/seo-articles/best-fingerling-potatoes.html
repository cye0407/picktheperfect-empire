Best Fingerling Potatoes

Fingerlings aren't just small potatoes. They're distinct varieties with different growing patterns, textures, and culinary properties.

Published: 2026-01-23

What Defines Fingerling Potatoes

Fingerling describes shape and size, but fingerling varieties share other consistent characteristics.

Shape and size

Fingerlings are elongated and small — typically 2-4 inches long. The shape isn't accidental; it's bred into the variety for generations.

Texture

Most fingerlings are waxy or firm rather than starchy. They hold their shape when cooked, making them suited to roasting and salads rather than mashing.

Flavor intensity

Fingerlings typically have more concentrated flavor than standard potatoes. The smaller size and waxy texture concentrate taste compounds.

Heritage background

Many fingerlings are heirloom varieties preserved from pre-industrial agriculture. They predate modern commercial breeding focused on size and yield.

Types of Fingerling Potatoes

French fingerlings

Classic European fingerlings with buttery flavor and waxy texture. They roast exceptionally well and are standard in bistro cooking.

Tradeoff: lower yield per plant than modern varieties. Heritage genetics prioritize quality over quantity.

Russian Banana

Yellow-fleshed fingerlings with distinctive flavor. They have good disease resistance for fingerlings and relatively reliable production.

Tradeoff: curved shape makes uniform cutting difficult. The irregular form affects presentation.

Purple fingerlings

Colorful fingerlings with purple or blue flesh. They add visual interest to dishes and have good antioxidant content.

Tradeoff: color can fade or turn gray when overcooked. Requires more careful cooking than standard varieties.

Growing Considerations for Fingerlings

Space efficiency

Fingerlings produce more tubers per plant but smaller individual sizes. Total weight per square foot is often similar to standard varieties despite different tuber count.

Container suitability

The small tuber size makes fingerlings well-suited to container growing. They tolerate crowding better than large russets.

Harvest timing

Many fingerlings are early or mid-season varieties. This reduces disease exposure but also limits storage potential compared to late-season types.

Culinary Uses for Fingerlings

Roasting whole

Fingerlings roast perfectly whole without cutting. Their shape creates ideal surface area for crisping, and the small size ensures even cooking.

Potato salad

The firm, waxy texture makes fingerlings excellent for potato salad. They hold shape beautifully and create visually distinctive presentation.

Pan-roasting with meat

Small size means fingerlings cook in the same time as chicken thighs or pork chops. They're ideal for one-pan dinners.

Common Fingerling Mistakes

Expecting high per-plant yield

Fingerlings produce many small tubers, not few large ones. Judging success by tuber count rather than weight creates false expectations.

Using them for mashing

Most fingerlings are waxy and resist mashing. They become gluey rather than fluffy. Use them for applications that benefit from firm texture.

Treating all fingerlings identically

"Fingerling" describes shape, not specific variety. Different fingerlings have different flavors, textures, and growing requirements.
