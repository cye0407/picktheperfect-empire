Best Baking Potatoes

Perfect baked potatoes require high-starch varieties with dry, fluffy texture. The variety determines the result more than baking technique.

Published: 2026-01-23

What Makes a Potato Ideal for Baking

Baking concentrates internal characteristics. The right variety creates light, fluffy interiors. Wrong varieties stay dense regardless of temperature or timing.

Starch content

Very high starch is essential. Starch granules separate during baking, creating the characteristic fluffy texture. Low-starch waxy potatoes remain dense and heavy when baked.

Moisture level

Dry potatoes create lighter baked texture. The low moisture allows starch cells to separate cleanly. Wet varieties steam internally and become dense.

Size

Larger potatoes bake more evenly. Small potatoes cook through before skin crisps properly. Medium-large size (8-12 oz) provides best balance of crispy skin and fluffy interior.

Skin thickness

Thick russet skins crisp beautifully when baked. Thin skins stay chewy. The skin texture matters for loaded baked potatoes where skin is eaten.

Potato Types That Excel for Baking

Russet Burbank

The standard baking potato. Very high starch, very low moisture, and perfect size create reliably fluffy results. Thick skin crisps well.

Tradeoff: relatively bland flavor. Depends heavily on toppings for taste.

Russet Norkotah

Similar to Burbank with slightly faster cooking. Good alternative where Burbank isn't available. Performs nearly identically in baking.

Tradeoff: slightly less fluffy than Burbank at peak performance. Still excellent for baking.

Large starchy specialty varieties

Some heritage varieties with russet-level starch content offer more flavor. They bake fluffy while providing more distinctive taste.

Tradeoff: less availability and often lower yields than commercial russets.

Common Baking Mistakes

Using waxy or all-purpose potatoes

Red potatoes and Yukon Golds bake acceptably but never achieve true fluffiness. They remain dense and creamy rather than light. No baking technique compensates for wrong variety.

Wrapping in foil

Foil traps moisture, steaming rather than baking the potato. This works against the variety's natural dryness. Even russets become dense when foil-wrapped.

Underbaking

Even perfect varieties need full cooking time. A partially baked russet stays dense in the center. Complete baking to 210°F internal temperature is necessary for maximum fluffiness.

The Size-Time Relationship

Larger potatoes need longer baking but produce better texture. A 12-ounce russet takes 75-90 minutes but achieves ideal fluffiness. Smaller potatoes cook faster but can't develop the same texture.

This isn't about patience. It's about allowing enough time for internal structural changes to complete.

Twice-Baked and Loaded Preparations

Structural requirements

Twice-baked potatoes need skins strong enough to hold fillings. Russets provide this. Thin-skinned varieties collapse when refilled.

Flavor absorption

High-starch potatoes absorb added ingredients better. The fluffy texture incorporates cheese, sour cream, and seasonings more effectively than dense varieties.
