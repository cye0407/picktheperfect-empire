Best Potatoes for Roasting

Great roasted potatoes come from varieties that brown well and maintain structure. It's about starch balance, not cooking time.

Published: 2026-01-23

What Actually Matters for Roasting

Roasting demands different qualities than mashing or frying. The best varieties are adapted to dry heat.

Starch-to-moisture ratio

Medium-starch potatoes roast best. Too much starch creates mealy interiors that fall apart. Too little prevents proper browning and crisping.

Sugar content

Natural sugars caramelize during roasting, creating the golden-brown exterior people expect. Low-sugar varieties stay pale and bland even with extended cooking.

Skin thickness

Thin-skinned varieties crisp faster and more evenly. Thick russet skins need higher heat or longer time to achieve the same texture.

Size consistency

Varieties that produce uniform-sized tubers roast more evenly. Mixed sizes mean some pieces burn while others stay undercooked.

Potato Types That Excel for Roasting

Yukon Gold and yellow varieties

Yellow potatoes hit the sweet spot — enough starch to crisp, enough moisture to stay creamy inside. Their natural butter flavor requires less seasoning.

Tradeoff: lower maximum crispiness than pure russets. The creamy interior comes with slightly softer edges.

Red potatoes

Red potatoes hold their shape reliably and develop good browning. Their thin skin crisps nicely without needing to be peeled.

Tradeoff: denser, less fluffy interior. They're firm rather than light.

Fingerlings

Fingerlings roast whole without cutting, creating maximum surface area for browning. Their small size means faster cooking and more crispy bits per pound.

Tradeoff: requires more prep time if cutting. Natural small size means less flexibility in portion control.

New potatoes

Young potatoes of any variety have thin skins and high sugar content, both beneficial for roasting. They crisp exceptionally well.

Tradeoff: seasonal availability. New potatoes are only available for limited windows after harvest.

Common Roasting Mistakes

Using high-starch russets exclusively

Russets can roast well but tend toward mealy interiors and uneven browning. They're optimized for baking and frying, not roasting. Medium-starch varieties perform more consistently.

Mixing potato types

Different varieties cook at different rates. Mixing waxy and starchy potatoes in one pan guarantees uneven results — some overcooked, some undercooked.

Choosing based on size rather than variety

"Small potatoes" describes many different types. A small russet roasts differently than a small fingerling. Variety matters more than size.

The Technique Dependency

Even ideal varieties need proper technique — adequate oil, high enough heat, and sufficient space in the pan. But no amount of technique fixes variety problems.

A high-starch potato will always be mealier than a medium-starch one, regardless of oil or temperature. A thick-skinned variety will always take longer to crisp than a thin-skinned one.

How the Picker Helps
